# Competitor routing workspace review

Date: 2026-08-04

## Evidence reviewed

- [Onfleet Command Center](https://support.onfleet.com/hc/en-us/articles/35183133144980-Command-Center): persistent route-day selection, quick operational statistics, attention-oriented route tables, customizable columns, synchronized route selection and map display, and planned-versus-actual timing.
- [Routific route planning tools](https://www.routific.com/route-planning-tools): synchronized map, timeline, and list views; direct drag-and-drop edits; route shaping; batch stop movement; driver reassignment; and an emphasis on simplicity.
- [Routific driver familiarity](https://help.routific.com/en/articles/45-driver-familiarity): completed-route history, proximity-based familiarity coverage, and visible familiarity bars beside drivers during route assignment.
- [OptimoRoute dispatcher guide](https://help.optimoroute.com/hc/en-us/articles/35511474016404-Getting-started-for-new-OptimoRoute-dispatchers): date-based planning, unscheduled work, map/timeline editing, best-fit insertion, driver and vehicle constraints, and planned-versus-actual live operations.
- [OptimoRoute breadcrumb guide](https://help.optimoroute.com/hc/en-us/articles/27442186795412-Use-breadcrumbs-to-track-drivers): planned, actual, and combined route traces; recorded time at a point; honest missing-breadcrumb causes; and historical review.
- [Onfleet completed-task routes](https://support.onfleet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360023669512-Completed-Tasks): completed route review with a draggable driver position, point timestamps, duration, and recorded distance.
- [Onfleet task assignment](https://support.onfleet.com/hc/en-us/articles/360023910111-Task-Assignment): Shift-drag selection of tasks inside a map region, multi-task assignment, and zone-aware selection.
- [Onfleet G2 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/onfleet/reviews): recurring praise for ease of use, a clean dispatcher dashboard, and real-time tracking.
- [Routific G2 reviews](https://www.g2.com/products/routific/reviews): recurring praise for ease of use, route optimization, and direct editing across the map, timeline, and list.
- [OptimoRoute Capterra reviews](https://www.capterra.com/p/161579/OptimoRoute/reviews/): recurring praise for fast planning, live tracking, and customer ETA notifications.
- [Routific customer notifications](https://help.routific.com/en/articles/66-how-to-set-up-sms-customer-notifications): separate scheduled, on-the-way, completed, and missed events; configurable lead time; and completion timing tied to the driver's recorded action.
- [OptimoRoute notification customization](https://help.optimoroute.com/hc/en-us/articles/27866949431060-Customize-real-time-order-tracking-notifications): email/text channel selection, scheduled/on-way/ETA-change/complete/failed events, configurable minutes before arrival, and an option to require the previous stop to finish.
- [OptimoRoute order-tracking configuration](https://help.optimoroute.com/hc/en-us/articles/27647646477972-Enable-and-configure-realtime-order-tracking-notifications): visible delivery status, one-send behavior for already-notified orders, proof-of-delivery presentation, and admin-managed customer communication settings.
- [Onfleet task completion](https://support.onfleet.com/hc/en-us/articles/10373142665364-Complete-a-Task): driver completion captures completion details including location, with signatures, photos, notes, and required fields available as proof controls.

## What Trovan already does well

- Three-column planner with unassigned work, a central map/timeline canvas, and route summaries.
- Selected, all-route, density, and exception map modes.
- Route timeline and drag-and-drop lane editing.
- Publish blockers, exception review, route workload metrics, and scenario comparison.
- Driver/vehicle assignment and a clear publish-to-dispatch handoff.

## Changes implemented from this review

1. Added a persistent route-day navigator with previous, next, today, and direct date selection. The selected day is written to the URL so refresh and shared links preserve planning context.
2. Added an attention-first route summary filter with All, Attention, and Ready views.
3. Replaced the synthetic route-capacity percentage with workload derived from planned route duration against an eight-hour route day.
4. Added explicit Ready/Attention route status and keyboard-operable route summary rows.
5. Made the unassigned-job sort functional for priority, customer, and city.
6. Removed placeholder mileage from unassigned jobs; the queue now states that mileage is awaiting route assignment instead of presenting invented distance.
7. Added a data-derived best-fit route recommendation for every unassigned job. Selecting a job highlights the suggested route and explains the route-area proximity and current workload so the dispatcher can review the fit before insertion.
8. Corrected the compact-layout breakpoint so tablet-sized planner windows use focused Map, Routes, and Jobs views instead of clipping the third desktop column.
9. Added a real best-fit insertion workflow. Desktop and compact job lists can insert an unassigned job into the recommended route; the backend enforces organization ownership, editable plan status, job activity and uniqueness, routing readiness, vehicle availability, and weight, volume, and shift constraints inside one transaction. Successful insertion resequences the lane, updates workload totals, returns the plan to draft, and requires reoptimization before publish.
10. Added multi-select route editing for desktop and compact workspaces. Dispatchers can select individual stops or every movable stop in a lane, choose a destination route, and move the group in one operation. Pickup and delivery stops for the same job stay together; protected stops, stale selections, split jobs, unavailable vehicles, and weight, volume, or shift violations are rejected transactionally. A successful move resequences every affected lane, updates route workload totals, clears stale planned times, returns the plan to draft, and requires reoptimization. Existing one-stop moves now use the same validated backend path.
11. Added an execution-priority dispatch view based on persisted planned and actual timestamps. Delayed and at-risk routes sort ahead of routine work; filters expose attention, delayed, at-risk, on-plan, and awaiting-data states; route cards show the exact comparison basis, planned time, confirmed or ETA time, and processed-stop progress. Dispatch readiness is now separate from schedule health, missing telemetry is labeled instead of inferred, the board actually refreshes every 30 seconds when auto-refresh is enabled, and the footer reports the real query update time. Vehicle markers now require reported vehicle coordinates rather than borrowing the first planned stop. The laptop layout uses two command columns and a full-width communications row, while wider screens retain three panes.
12. Added configurable route-summary columns and saved dispatcher views. Dispatchers can choose among assignment, stop, mileage, duration, service, weight, volume, and workload fields; apply built-in Operations, Attention, and Capacity workflows; and save, update, rename, restore, or delete up to 12 personal views. A saved view restores its columns, summary filter, route search, quick filter, driver and vehicle filters, and map mode. Preferences remain scoped by organization and user, reject malformed stored data, and intentionally exclude route-day and route-selection state. The same workflow is available in the compact Routes tab without horizontal overflow.
13. Added telemetry freshness, historical breadcrumbs, and route-position replay. The tracking workspace now sorts stale and delayed signals ahead of healthy vehicles; labels Live (at most two minutes), Delayed (at most ten minutes), Stale, and Unavailable states; and reports measured fleet signal coverage instead of a synthetic adherence score. Selecting a vehicle can compare Planned, Actual, or Both traces, switch among 1-hour, 6-hour, and 24-hour history, scrub a recorded position, or play it forward with timestamp and reported speed. The organization-scoped backend contract bounds history to 1–168 hours and the most recent 1,000 points, returns chronological metadata, and denies foreign vehicle IDs before querying telemetry. Malformed coordinates and timestamps are discarded. Production never builds a breadcrumb from planned stops when telemetry is missing; the synthetic breadcrumb generator runs only in explicitly labeled preview mode.
14. Added geographic map-area selection for unassigned work. Dispatchers enter an explicit Draw area mode, drag a freehand polygon around a cluster, and review the selected count, high-priority mix, excluded unlocated jobs, and workload-aware best-fit route before changing the plan. Only unassigned jobs with persisted coordinates can be selected; normal map panning is restored immediately after the gesture. The cluster can be cleared without mutation or inserted into the recommended route through the existing organization, plan-state, job-readiness, vehicle, capacity, and shift constraint checks. Every successful insertion returns the plan to draft and retains the requirement to reoptimize before publish.
15. Added evidence-backed driver familiarity during route assignment. Trovan compares each located stop in the selected draft route with serviced, geocoded stops from the organization’s completed routes during the prior 365 days. A historical visit counts only within 2 km, and a driver becomes recommendation-eligible only after at least two completed routes and five serviced located stops. Eligible drivers receive zero-to-three coverage bars in the assignment list; the best history match includes its coverage percentage, completed-route count, and nearby-visit count plus a one-click assignment action. Missing coordinates, insufficient history, or API errors produce explicit learning/unavailable states instead of inferred expertise. Historical routes, active drivers, and job-stop locations are organization-scoped before scoring; the synthetic demonstration runs only in labeled preview mode.
16. Added admin-controlled, email-first customer notification timing and completion-location review. Settings now expose scheduled, on-the-way, completed, and failed events; an on-the-way lead time from 5 to 180 minutes; an option to wait for the previous stop; and a 25-to-2,000-meter completion review threshold. A minute scheduler considers only in-progress routes and the next eligible stop, while non-ETA events reuse existing organization/route/job/channel delivery records so replanning or scheduler repeats do not create duplicate sends. SMS remains visibly disabled for the assisted-pilot launch unless its server feature flag and provider are both configured. When a driver completes a stop, Trovan compares the planned job-stop coordinate with vehicle telemetry no more than five minutes old, stores the evidence with the completion event, and opens a `COMPLETION_LOCATION_VARIANCE` exception outside the configured radius. Route detail shows verified, review, and unavailable states and explicitly identifies the source as vehicle telemetry rather than claiming driver-device GPS. Organization, job, customer, route, stop, driver, job-stop, and telemetry reads remain tenant-scoped where tenant context exists.
17. Closed final desktop discoverability and map-control gaps found by the exhaustive interaction audit. The selected-route inspector, including driver familiarity, now remains available in the desktop right rail instead of appearing only in compact mode. The geographic Draw area control no longer overlaps the Satellite/Streets switch, route-timeline stop controls use stable descriptive names instead of clock-dependent labels, and manual telemetry refresh reports when the check completes. Preview familiarity is generated from the active route-day groups, stops, and drivers so the demonstration cannot silently show history for unrelated seed routes. Map viewport fits are non-animated to remain stable across rapid route transitions without introducing teardown work after React unmounts the map.

## Highest-value remaining gaps

1. Add opt-out, message-template, and bounded ETA-change notification controls after provider-backed pilot usage proves the email workflow; do not enable SMS until consent, STOP handling, and delivery operations are ready.
2. Capture driver-device completion GPS as a separately labeled source when the driver client has an authenticated upload contract; keep vehicle telemetry honest and unavailable when it is stale.
3. After pilot operating history, consider a dedicated atomic multi-job insertion endpoint so a large geographic batch either commits completely or leaves the route plan unchanged.
4. Add administrator-managed driver service areas and skills only when pilot customers need hard assignment constraints; keep historical familiarity advisory rather than silently turning it into a restriction.

These are product gaps, not reasons to imitate competitor branding or visual identity. Trovan should retain its own design system while adopting the proven dispatcher interaction patterns.
